About

Most of my career starts the same way: a black t-shirt or hoodie, a new role in an unfamiliar industry or market sector, and a team that needs me contributing from day one.

From an early-stage startup (later acquired) to finance, telecom, and serving as the point of contact for Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA) subsidiary strategy and risk governance, I’ve repeatedly walked in with the most to learn, and been up to speed and driving results within weeks. That track record includes unlocking $13.4M in new revenue potential at a Series C startup, delivering a $3.46M network modernization a year ahead of schedule, and giving VPs and Directors monthly risk and strategy briefings.

There's no real secret to it. When I start something new, I get a little obsessive. I read everything I can find and spend my spare time learning how the whole business works, not just my corner of it. Eventually the unfamiliar starts to feel familiar, and I usually find that the underlying operational problems are ones I've already solved, just wearing different clothes. For the teams I join, that means fewer blind spots and fewer wheels reinvented.

That habit doesn't switch off between jobs. It’s what pushed me to earn an MBA and a technical master’s. Right now, my evenings and weekends go to relearning electrical engineering and hardware, and during the week I use AI tools like Claude Code and Amazon Kiro for rapid prototyping and building workflows used by teams and executives.

The hardware part isn't random. My first job was on the build side: PCB design, soldering, and helping put physical products together. I miss those days: the fast pace, getting my hands dirty, and being close to the build. I want to bring my Strategy & Operations playbook back to supporting engineering teams making real things.

I'm at my best where the ground is still moving and the job is to act as a force multiplier for executives, figuring things out faster than expected and turning strategy into execution. If that sounds like what you're building, especially at a smaller, fast-paced company, I'd love to connect.

How I work

I'm a leader who is data-driven with a strategic vision. The numbers tell me what's true, and the strategy decides what's worth doing about it.

Hands-off, with a clear rhythm

I manage teams with a light touch. The team and I meet twice a week to level-set expectations and goals for the current week and the one ahead, and then people own their work.

Hands-on when it counts

When something needs to get done, I get hands-on-keyboard and work alongside the team instead of directing from the sidelines.

Start with the data

Before I form an opinion, I get to the facts: the baseline, the trend, and the few metrics that actually drive the outcome.

Keep the long view

Data shows where things stand. A clear strategy decides where to go, and which of the many things we could do are worth doing.

Learn the whole business

I read everything I can and learn how the entire business works, not just my corner of it, so there are fewer blind spots and fewer wheels reinvented.

Simplify until it's obvious

With Amazon Kiro, I built an internal tool that lets executive leaders overseeing a Fortune 500 division track manufacturing milestones in real time. I cut it down to the few milestones that matter and their live status, so leaders see where things stand without waiting for a report. I prototype with AI tools like Claude Code and Kiro so teams react to something real.
